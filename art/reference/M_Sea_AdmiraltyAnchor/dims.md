# M_Sea_AdmiraltyAnchor (S1a)
dims_m: 3.35 x 4.0 x 1.93
Class: 1841 Admiralty-pattern (Sir William Parker's) anchor, iron-stocked, merchant bower of about 15 to 20 cwt
(0.8 to 1.0 t) for a 200 to 300 ton brig or barque. Proportions of the pattern: stock length = shank length, arm span
(bill to bill) about 0.6 x shank, palms about 0.45 x arm. Shank 3.2 m (10.5 ft) + shackle and ring (4.0 m overall), stock 3.2 m + ball ends (3.35 m), arm span 1.9 m.
Pose: lying with the stock flat on the seabed and the arms upright (the usual seabed attitude of a stocked anchor).
SIZE NOTE: WorldConfig gives 15 x 9 studs (5 x 3 m). A 5 m shank is a ship-of-the-line best bower (about 90 cwt, 4.5 t,
shank about 17 to 18 ft), out of place by a fishing-harbour wall; and its own proportions would make it 5 x 5 m, not 5 x 3.
Recommend WorldConfig.POIs AdmiraltyAnchor length 12, beam 10 studs (4.0 x 3.35 m) at integration.
Sources (reference only, not shipped):

- Royal Museums Greenwich, Admiralty pattern anchors (collections search "Admiralty pattern anchor"): https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections (verify)
- Weight to shank-length rule (L proportional to W^(1/3), 90 cwt -> about 18 ft): Lever, "The Sheet Anchor" (1808/1819) tables (verify)
Earlier board notes (kept): a c. 1750 example at the National Maritime Museum is about 4.9 m long and just under 3 m
across the flukes, about 2,000 to 2,300 kg (41 to 45 cwt, Big Anchor project tables): https://rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-18191
That is a large-ship anchor, which is why WorldConfig's 5 x 3 m is shrunk here; catalogue range 1 to 40 cwt.
